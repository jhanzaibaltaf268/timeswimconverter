However, international galas take place exclusively in 50-metre Long Course Meters (LCM) or 25-metre Short Course Meters (SCM), while US college dual meets are swum in 25-yard Short Course Yards (SCY). ## Why LCM to SCY Conversion for International Recruits Makes Swimmers Faster

When converting an LCM (50m) time to an SCY (25yd) time:

1. **Distance Reduction**: A 100m race is $100\text{ metres}$, while a 100yd race is $91.44\text{ metres}$—a $8.56\text{ metre}$ drop in total distance. Calculate exact distances with our [Yards to Meters Swimming Converter](/yards-to-meters/).
2. **Double the Turns**: In a 100m LCM race, you get **1 turn** (2 lengths). In a 100yd SCY race, you get **3 turns** (4 lengths).

Gaining extra wall push-offs and reducing overall race distance results in **significantly faster SCY times**.

## Key Advice for International Recruits Contacting US Coaches

1. **Include Both Actual and Converted Times**: In your recruiting email, list your official FINA/World Aquatics LCM time first, followed by the converted SCY estimated time using our [USA Swimming Time Converter](/swim-time-usa/).
2. **Highlight Underwater Turn Skill**: If you have strong dolphin kicks off walls, point this out—college coaches know turn-dominant swimmers transition exceptionally well to 25-yard pools.
3. **Verify Long-Course Meet Licensing**: Ensure your LCM times were swum in officially licensed level 1 or level 2 galas so coaches can verify them on national databases.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will my LCM time automatically translate to a fast SCY time?
Usually yes. However, international swimmers who rely purely on surface stroke power and have weak flip turns may experience a learning curve adapting to the high turn frequency of 25-yard pools. Check race splits using our [Swim Split Calculator](/split-calculator/).

### Do US college coaches recruit international swimmers for LCM meets?
Yes. Although the NCAA college season is SCY, major international meets and the summer Olympic Trials are LCM. NCAA coaches value recruits who excel in both short-course yards and long-course meters.
