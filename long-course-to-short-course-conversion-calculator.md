## Turn Density: The Engine of Short-Course Speed

The primary reason short-course times are faster is **turn density**. A wall push-off produces an initial launch velocity between **$2.5\text{ m/s}$ and $3.5\text{ m/s}$**, compared to steady-state surface swimming velocities of **$1.6\text{ m/s}$ to $1.9\text{ m/s}$**.

```
50m LCM (1 Lap, 0 Turns): [Dive ──► Breakout ──────────────────────── Surface Swim ────────────────────────► Touch]
25m SCM (2 Laps, 1 Turn):  [Dive ──► Breakout ──► Surface] ──► [Flip Turn ──► Breakout ──► Surface ──► Touch]
```

In a 200m event:
- **50m LCM**: 3 turns total.
- **25m SCM**: 7 turns total (**4 extra turns** = $\approx 2.4\text{s} - 3.6\text{s}$ gained).
- **25yd SCY**: 7 turns total over 200 yards (**182.88m** total distance).

## Conversion Equations: LCM ➔ SCM & LCM ➔ SCY

### 1. Long Course (LCM) to Short Course Meters (SCM)
$$T_{\text{SCM}} = T_{\text{LCM}} - \left( N_{\text{added turns}} \times W_{\text{gain}} \right)$$

Where $N_{\text{added turns}}$ is the additional turns gained in a 25m pool, and $W_{\text{gain}}$ is the wall bonus ($\approx 0.50\text{s} - 0.85\text{s}$ depending on stroke discipline).

### 2. Long Course (LCM) to Short Course Yards (SCY)
$$T_{\text{SCY}} = \left( T_{\text{LCM}} - (N_{\text{added turns}} \times W_{\text{gain}}) \right) \times 0.900$$

*(Incorporates the 9.36% distance reduction from 100 meters to 100 yards).*

## Worked Conversion Example: 200m Freestyle

Suppose an athlete records an Olympic Trials qualifying time of **`1:50.00`** in the 200m Freestyle LCM:

1. **Calculate Extra Turns**: 200m LCM has 3 turns; 200m SCM has 7 turns ($4$ extra turns).
2. **Apply Wall Advantage**: $4 \times 0.70\text{s} = 2.80\text{s}$ time reduction.
3. **Projected SCM Time**: $1:50.00 - 2.80\text{s} = \mathbf{1:47.20}$.
4. **Project to SCY (200 Yards)**: Multiply SCM by $0.900$ distance ratio:
   $$107.20\text{s} \times 0.900 = 96.48\text{s} = \mathbf{1:36.48\text{ (SCY)}}.$$

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do long-course times drop so significantly in short course?
The combination of extra wall push-offs (which are faster than surface swimming) and the opportunity to execute high-speed underwater dolphin kicks allows swimmers to carry greater momentum throughout the race.

### Is long-course swimming physically harder than short-course swimming?
Yes. Without regular wall push-offs to break up surface swimming, athletes experience higher continuous drag, greater shoulder and core fatigue, and fewer opportunities for brief micro-rests during turns.

### How accurate is the long course to short course conversion calculator for breaststrokers?
Breaststroke experiences the largest time drops when moving to short course because the underwater pullout off each turn generates immense propulsion compared to surface breaststroke mechanics.
